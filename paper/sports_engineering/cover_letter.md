Dear Editors,

Please consider our Research Article, “Calibration-free two-view 3D pose
fusion for gymnastics motion analysis and repeated-cycle assessment,” for
publication in *Sports Engineering*.

This manuscript addresses a practical sports-engineering problem: how to
combine two temporally aligned monocular three-dimensional pose sequences when
camera calibration and independent three-dimensional training labels are
unavailable. The proposed workflow transforms face- and side-view poses into a
pelvis-centred body frame and applies a view-order-invariant, bounded temporal
correction trained with rotation, skeletal and temporal self-supervision.

The manuscript should interest readers working on markerless sports motion
analysis for three reasons. First, it evaluates a low-infrastructure
post-estimation workflow rather than assuming access to calibrated cameras.
Second, it reports both a private same-video pseudo-reference comparison and a
limited external native-three-dimensional test, including the negative
external-transfer result. Third, it demonstrates person-disjoint use of the
fused sequences for between-cohort and within-person repeated-cycle analyses,
with sensitivity checks showing that the observed speed and jerk contrasts
depend on the pose representation.

The main conclusion is deliberately bounded. Pelvis-centred canonicalization
accounts for most of the in-domain improvement; the complete learned model is
statistically indistinguishable from strong held-out baselines and does not
replace calibrated reconstruction. The cohort analysis is presented as
exploratory and observational, not as a causal or clinical finding. We believe
this transparent distinction between an engineering representation and a
validated biomechanical measurement fits the applied scope of *Sports
Engineering*.

The main body contains approximately 2,330 words, 2 figures and 3 tables. One
Online Resource contains detailed ablations, corruption settings, robustness
results and secondary statistics. The manuscript is not under consideration
elsewhere, and the author will confirm all required declarations in the
submission system.
